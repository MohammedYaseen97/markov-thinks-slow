## Current state of knowledge

This area has been quiet in both windows so far. Nothing has been ingested as a full source. The notes below are pointers, not findings.

- Transfer of training in humans (2026-10-08, a post by François Chollet, no primary source read): 1980s research on the "cognitive consequences of programming" expected programming to improve general reasoning. According to the post, the original studies and later meta-analyses found gains in algorithmic thinking that did not transfer to unrelated problem solving. He uses it to ask whether RL with verifiable rewards on math and code lifts general ability or only those domains.
- Miller Lab (2026-10-04, post only): prefrontal beta strengthened with learning and tracked reward expectations.
- Harness design framed from the human side (Paras Chopra, 2026-10-08): real work is ill-specified and people discover what they want as they go, so harnesses should be designed around the human's cognition.

## Open questions

- Is there a human analogue of the token-cue result, where a small contextual trigger decides whether deliberate reasoning is engaged at all? Dual-process theory would predict one.
- What does the transfer literature say about which kinds of practice do generalize?
